MODEL & EVIDENCE

Every priority has a reason.

Crash Cart uses the existing InfraBench heuristic. Fleet profiles are illustrative populations; they are not a connected inventory or a live monitoring feed.

  1. Start with published lifetime failure rates. Each model has a rate and an observation volume from Backblaze Drive Stats.
  2. Adjust for age and SMART evidence. The existing age curve and likelihood ratios turn a base annual rate into an adjusted rate.
  3. Convert to a 30-day probability. P(failure) = 1 − exp(−annual rate × 30 / 365). An annualized rate is not itself a probability.
  4. Rank by population exposure. Drive count × 30-day failure probability × affected capacity. This is not expected data loss.

What your plan means

Selected exposure = population exposure × selected quantity / population size. This assumes equal per-drive exposure inside a population. It measures which part of the score your plan addresses; it does not claim that replacement erases that risk.

“Fill by model priority” follows the original population ranking and allocates capacity from the top down. It is not an optimization for maximum exposure per individual replacement. The curve uses that same ordering.

Constraints and uncertainty

The model does not know drive locations, shared RAID groups, maintenance windows, replacement stock, or the temporary risk introduced by a planned rebuild. Its narrow published-rate intervals do not capture uncertainty in the age, evidence, or topology assumptions.
