Under the hood

Each row of the pull list is one batch of drives carried through the same four steps. The example follows whatever is at the top of your list.

  1. Start from the published rate

    Every model begins at its lifetime failure rate from Backblaze Drive Stats, with the drive days behind it, so a thin record shows as a wide interval rather than false certainty.

  2. Adjust for age

    Failure rates follow a bathtub: a bump in the first months, a floor near year two, then a climb that gets serious after year five. Each batch takes the multiplier for its own age.

  3. Weigh the SMART evidence

    In the public fleet, 77 percent of failed drives showed one of five SMART warnings, against 4 percent of healthy ones. A flagged drive is about 18 times likelier to fail; a clean one, a quarter as likely.

  4. Weigh what a failure costs

    Every failure starts a rebuild that exposes its whole group. Multiply the chance of failure by the data sitting behind it and the list sorts itself: risk times blast radius.

This is the whole model. If you disagree with a constant, you know exactly which one to argue with.

Annual rate
lifetime rate × age multiplier × evidence ratio
Evidence ratio
18.26 if flagged, 0.24 if clean normalized to the coverage you set
Chance of failing in 30 days
1 − exp(−annual rate × 30 / 365)
Rebuild hours
capacity TB × 106 / rebuild MB/s / 3600
Exposure per survivor
1 − exp(−batch rate × rebuild hours / 8760)
Chance a group loses data
P(at least m of the k+m−1 survivors fail in that window)
Blast radius
k × capacity the data the group holds
At risk, 30 days
drives × chance of failing × blast radius the sort key
Data actually lost
the same × chance the group loses data the fleet total

SMART is evidence, not a threshold. Flagged drives carry an 18× likelihood ratio and clean ones 0.24×, renormalized to the coverage you set so the fleet’s total expected failures are preserved.

The age multiplier follows the published bathtub shape, normalized so a mixed-age fleet averages 1.0. Look ahead adds the same years to every batch and reads the curve at the new age: no replacements, no new drives, no change in rates beyond the curve.

Rebuild rate is the effective rate on a live system, not the spec sheet; 150 MB/s is an honest default. Unrecoverable read errors count only where no parity is left.

The list holds drives with a reason. The bands sit at 1.5 percent a year (watch), 5 (schedule) and 15 (pull). The crew works the list top down; weeks after the first assume it also absorbs the drives that newly earn a place each week, which is what the plan chart and the week scrubber show.

A triage heuristic, not a warranty model. Figures are the published lifetime table, rounded; re-pull the current quarter before you spend money, and nothing here replaces watching your own fleet.
