Infrabench
About GitHub

Published specs, corrected for reality.

A rack is rated for 600 kW, a drive for five years, an interconnection queue in gigawatts. None of it survives contact with a real site. Infrabench is a set of free tools that correct those numbers against public data, with every constant on the page.

  1. Rack Budget

    You have the megawatts. What actually fits?

    Power, cooling and floor area for an AI hall. Start from a megawatt budget or from a fleet size and get the other side, plus the heat you have to reject.

    Data: NVIDIA platform disclosures, Uptime Institute PUE survey

    Open tool: Rack Budget→

  2. Crash Cart

    You cannot pull them all. Which ones this week?

    Drive failure triage on thirteen years of public fleet telemetry: a live pull list, ranked by risk times blast radius, that replays week by week.

    Data: Backblaze Drive Stats, about 300,000 drives

    Open tool: Crash Cart→

  3. Where the power is

    The chips are the easy part. Where is the power?

    Siting under a power constraint. Ten US grid regions ranked by the earliest realistic date a utility can energize your load, drawn against your deadline.

    Data: LBNL Queued Up, EPA eGRID, EIA

    Open tool: Where the power is→

  4. Outage replay

    The dashboard was green. How long had it been wrong?

    Six public cloud incidents replayed against the provider’s own retrospective: what the status page said, and how long after customers started failing.

    Data: retrospectives from AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Cloudflare and Fastly

    Open tool: Outage replay→

How the tools are built

Public data only. Every input is something you could download yourself. Nothing proprietary, nothing behind an NDA, nothing you have to take on trust.

Every constant on the page. Each tool states its assumptions and shows the arithmetic behind every number. If you disagree with a constant, you know exactly which one to argue with.

One decision out. Each tool ends by naming the thing that will actually stop you, because that is the part someone has to act on.
