      02Why the tool exists

      Three things a spec sheet will not tell you

      Buying guides compare benchmark scores. Nobody runs a benchmark for eight thousand hours a year, and nobody ships a chart of what fits in 16 GB.

      01

      Idle writes the bill

      A machine used three hours a day spends the other twenty one drawing something. For most home setups the always-on floor, not the peak, is the larger half of the annual kilowatt hours.

      02

      Memory decides, bandwidth paces

      Whether a model runs at all is a capacity question. How fast it answers is a bandwidth question. They are different numbers on the box and they fail in different ways.

      03

      The breaker is a real limit

      A 575 W card, two monitors and a laser printer share one 15 A circuit rated for 1,440 W continuous. Nothing on the product page mentions the other things already plugged into that wall.

      03How it works

      Four inputs, in the order they actually get decided

      The set first, because it is the only thing you choose. Then the hours, the rate and the wall, because those are the ones people guess at and get wrong by a factor of three.

      Step 00 / The set

      Build it the way it will actually sit

      Not just the interesting box. The router, the NAS and the second monitor are the ones that never turn off, and they are usually the surprise on the bill. Start from a kit and edit it.

      Step 01 / The hours

      Be honest about the duty cycle

      Under load is the hours the hardware is working. On but idle is the hours it is awake and doing nothing. Anything marked always on keeps drawing for the rest of the day whatever you set here.

      Step 02 / The rate

      Use your own tariff

      18.44 cents is the US residential average. California and much of Europe run more than double that, which moves a 300 W idle floor from an annoyance to a reason to buy different hardware.

      Step 03 / The wall

      Pick the circuit it plugs into

      Peak draw is compared against the branch circuit, derated to 80 percent on the North American options because that is what continuous load means in the code. If you are over, you find out here.

      Worked example, the local-AI desktop kit at default hours
      Tower with an RTX 5090, one 32 inch display, a router
      → at load = 120 + 500 + 45 + 10 = 675 W
      → 3 h at load, 5 h idle at 70.5 W, router always on = 2.55 kWh per day
      → times 365 at 18.44 cents = 929 kWh and 171 dollars a year, a fifth of it spent idling
      → peak 200 + 575 + 65 + 12 = 852 W against a 1,440 W continuous ceiling = 59 percent of the circuit
      → 32 GB of VRAM, less 1 GB for display and context = 31.0 GB usable
      → Qwen3 32B at Q4 is 18.0 GB of weights, 18.9 with overhead, plus 1.0 GB of cache at 8K = 19.9 GB, fits
      → 1,792 GB/s x 0.75 efficiency / 18.0 GB read per token = about 75 tokens a second
      The surprise is rarely the GPU. It is that the same set burns about 190 kWh a year while nobody is sitting at it, and that Llama 3.3 70B at Q4 misses the 31.0 GB ceiling by 11.6 GB no matter how fast the card is.

      06The method

      Every constant, in the open

      A buying tool that hides its assumptions is a shop. Here is the whole model. If you disagree with a constant, you now know exactly which one to argue with.

      The model10 equations
      Draw at loadsum of qty × load watts
      Daily energyload W × load h + idle W × idle h, in kWh
      Always-on idleidle W × (24 − load h) for that gear
      Annual costdaily kWh × 365 × rate
      Heat at loadload W × 3.412 BTU per hour
      Circuit loadpeak W / (volts × amps × derate)
      Usable memoryunified × platform share, or VRAM − 1 GB
      Model footprintparams × bits / 8, ×1.05, plus KV cache
      Generation speedbandwidth × 0.75 / bytes read per token
      Energy per tokenload W / tokens per second, in watt hours
      The judgement calls7 notes
      • Generation is memory bound, not compute bound. One token at a time reads every active weight once, so bandwidth divided by weight size is the ceiling. The 0.75 covers real-world overhead; measured results land between 0.6 and 0.85.
      • That ceiling is for generation only. Reading your prompt is compute bound and much faster, so a slow machine still feels slow in exactly one place: the words appearing.
      • Two cards double the memory, not the speed. Split by layer, one card computes while the other waits, so bandwidth stays roughly that of a single card.
      • Unified memory is not all yours. macOS hands about 75 percent to the GPU by default, the Ryzen AI Max pool addresses about 96 of its 128 GB, and a discrete card loses about 1 GB to context and display.
      • Quantisation is not free. Q4_K_M is charged at 4.5 bits per weight and Q8_0 at 8.5, including the scales. Quality falls with the bits, most visibly below Q4.
      • Heat equals power. Essentially all electricity into a device leaves as heat. In summer the air conditioner pays for it twice.
      • A planning heuristic, not a benchmark. Silicon lottery, thermals, drivers and firmware move every one of these numbers. Measure your own with a plug meter before spending real money.
